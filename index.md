# Tempo as a Means of Understanding Time in PT's Filmography

## What is Tempo?

Tempo is most often thought of as a characteristic of music. Tempo measures the pace of a song through beats per minute (BPM). However, tempo can be used to understand the rhythm of a film. But what does this look like? [Brianica]

## Tempo in Film:
Within films there are moments that feel noticeably faster or slower than the rest of the film. This variation in perceived time is a tool used by filmmakers for a multitude of reasons. An example of these could be: action scenes with lots of dynamic movement within the shots and quick cutting; the moment before someone gets caught by a killer in a horror movie draws out the audience’s anxiety making them feel more on edge; or a round of consecutive quick scenes to convey a passage of time to the audience (like in training montages). [the way hollywood sees it]

## How Can We Measure Tempo in Film?
Many techniques can be employed to help change the tempo of a film: shots with lots of fast movement captured (actors running, objects moving fast, disco strobe), intense and dynamic camera movement (whiplash pans, jerky reframings, rack focusing), or quick cutting (making the length of each shot super short). The data in this feature uses the average shot length (ASL) to measure the tempo of PTA’s films. [the way hollywood sees it, distant viewing explorer]
